A sharp acetone or nail polish smell is very common in a young starter, and it usually means the culture is hungry rather than ruined. Around day three to five the first wave of bacteria dies back as the acidity rises, and the yeast that will eventually leaven your bread has not yet taken over, so the jar can look quiet for a while. Here is what I would change. First, feed it on a fixed schedule: discard all but about fifty grams, then add fifty grams of flour and fifty grams of water. Second, keep it warm, ideally between twenty-four and twenty-seven degrees, because a cold kitchen slows everything down. Third, use at least some whole grain flour, such as whole wheat or rye, since it carries more wild yeast and nutrients than plain white flour. Finally, be patient: many starters need ten to fourteen days before they reliably double within eight hours after a feeding.
